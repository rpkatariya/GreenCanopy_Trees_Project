Major Forest Ecosystems Across the Globe

Depending on latitude, temperature, and annual rainfall, forests organize into distinct biomes—each adapted to unique climate conditions.

Coastal Guardians: India's Sundarbans Mangrove Forest acts as a natural buffer wall that absorbs storm surges and cyclones along the Bay of Bengal.

Four Primary Forest Biomes

  • Tropical Evergreen Rainforests: Found near the equator and India's Western Ghats, receiving over 200 cm of annual rain with dense multi-layered canopies.
  • Tropical Deciduous (Monsoon) Forests: India's most widespread forest type, where teak and sal trees shed their leaves during the dry summer to conserve water.
  • Himalayan Coniferous Forests: High-altitude pine, deodar, and fir forests with needle-like leaves and cone seeds adapted to heavy snowfall.
  • Tidal Mangrove Forests: Salt-tolerant trees with stilt roots growing along river deltas and coastal estuaries.
