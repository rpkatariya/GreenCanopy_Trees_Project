Anatomy of a Living Tree

Every tree is a self-sustaining biological tower built from specialized tissues that anchor it in the soil, transport water upward against gravity, and convert sunlight into sugar.

Nature's Time Capsule: Counting the concentric growth rings inside a tree trunk reveals not only the tree's exact age, but also whether past years had heavy monsoons or severe droughts.

Major Structural Parts

  • Root System: Anchors the trunk against high winds and absorbs water and dissolved minerals from deep soil layers.
  • Heartwood & Sapwood (Xylem): Dense inner heartwood provides structural strength, while outer sapwood carries water from the roots up to the leaves.
  • Cambium & Phloem: A microscopic growing layer that creates new wood each year and carries glucose sugars downward from the canopy.
  • Outer Bark: A tough, corky shield that protects the living inner tissues from insects, extreme heat, and fungal infections.
